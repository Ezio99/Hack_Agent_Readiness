## 4. Does Kitchen behave as the predicted negative control? No — not cleanly
The pre-registered prediction was |Δ visits| < 0.5 and |Δ requests| ≤ 1 (B − A). Observed Δ visits −0.67 and Δ requests −2.0, **in the opposite direction to the hypothesis** (arm A cost more). All six runs passed and every B run used exactly the minimum (1 visit), so the control does not show any B penalty, which is the main point; but it also shows that **run-to-run variability in arm A is as large as the effect sizes being compared**: the Kitchen A-cell cost range (US$0.095) exceeds the Home B − A cost difference (+US$0.055), and Kitchen's A-minus-B difference (+US$0.028) has the opposite sign to Home's. The Home contrast (B costlier) therefore cannot yet be attributed to the manipulation; it remains consistent with the prediction but is not separated from noise. Reasons for A's excess here were behavioural, not structural (below). Statistics are exploratory; nothing in the analysis plan is confirmatory.

## 5. Strategies, re-verification, guesses and loops
- **No guesses, no wrong or unverified answers, no non-target product pages** in any run. All six identified Glass Carafe correctly.
- **Arm B (3/3, overhead 0):** each applied the Kitchen category filter (select + "Apply filters"), opened Glass Carafe, then went back to the filtered list and answered — a listing re-check after the detail page, but no extra detail visit. So B also "re-verifies" at the listing level, though never at the detail level.
- **Arm A:** read availability and the correct answer from the listing in all three runs, then differed. Run 09 (minimal): click, go back, done (overhead 0). Run 08: after the first detail visit it navigated to the root listing and applied the Kitchen filter "to be thorough" (doubting that 20 products might hide more), reopened Glass Carafe a second time, then went back and answered (+1). Run 12: **a tool-use loop** — six consecutive `find_elements` calls (repeatedly requesting the `href` attribute) before clicking the link, then a detour to the root listing and a second visit to Glass Carafe (+1; 10 requests, the costliest run of the installment). The loop is a model/tool-usage failure unrelated to the page design but it inflated arm A's effort and cost.
- **Secondary-finding update (for the earlier hypothesis that arm A re-verifies unnecessarily while B follows the minimum-visit path):** the pattern held for detail visits again (A overhead +1 in 2 of 3 Kitchen runs, B overhead 0 in 3 of 3; across both scopes A 4 of 6 runs with +1, B 0 of 6), and there was **no case in either arm of fewer pages than required**. But B also returns to the listing to re-check, and the A revisits were driven by "be thorough / check the full catalog" reasoning and a tool loop rather than by any use of availability information. Still a hypothesis, n = 6 per arm over two scopes.
